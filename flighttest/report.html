EC135 Simulated Flight Test

No test pilot was available, so the flight test was split into three roles that never saw each other's work. An author wrote down, from published sources only, what a real EC135 P2+ does in 19 manoeuvres. A scripted pilot flew exactly those manoeuvres in the simulator. An evaluator, who saw the expectations and the recordings but not the model, judged every expectation.

Each flight was judged by a new evaluator who saw neither the model nor the earlier verdicts. The cards stayed the same; between the flights the model and the test pilot changed (below). Evaluators differ at the margins: on the same or nearly the same recording an expectation has been rated PASS by one and NEAR by the next.

The model is configured with the correct counterclockwise rotor, and every rotation-dependent sign on the cards is right: left skid low in the hover, left pedal in the hover and right pedal in cruise and autorotation, nose right with collective, nose left at power loss, Fenestron unloading with right pedal, left-side cyclic through translational lift, and retreating-blade stall on the left with a left roll. Most dynamics land inside the author's bands. Hover roll and pitch responses to cyclic, the unstable hover phugoid (13 s period, about 5 s to double), heave with a 2.5 s time constant, translational lift, the power bucket (minimum about 63 kt), the forward-flight short period, the Dutch roll (2.5 s period, damping ratio 0.32), positive speed stability, roll control at low g and coordinated-turn kinematics are all realistic. The vortex-ring cards are a highlight: collective alone loses 843 ft with irregular 29 % thrust surges, while the classical recovery loses only 126 ft. The weak spots are in yaw and rotor-speed behaviour. In the hover power loss the rotor keeps decaying for about 2 s after the lever is fully down, with NR only 75.8 % at 5 s, which trips a red flag. Hover yaw is strongly coupled to sideways drift: cyclic pulses produce 7-9 deg/s of uncommanded yaw, and after a pedal step the yaw rate stalls at about 25 deg/s instead of decaying. The roll-in at 100 kt shows slight proverse rather than adverse yaw. Power and collective climb more steeply toward 135 kt relative to the hover than predicted (P135/Phover 1.32; collective 4.0 deg above hover). The stall roll at 140 kt is about 60 deg, larger than the predicted 5-35 deg. The model trims at about 3.5 deg sideslip with the ball centred at 80-100 kt. Two transients outside the cards are worth knowing about. In F6 the first 2.3 s after the failure produce a 34.5 deg left bank, 18 deg of nose-down pitch and -0.23 g (rotor thrust briefly negative), and the autopilot needed full aft and full right cyclic to recover. In F5 a pure +5 m/s speed step yawed the nose left and produced a 20 deg left bank within 6 s. Both are physically coherent (sideslip-driven dihedral roll, nose-down on lever reduction), but they are large.

Between the flights

The cards stayed frozen (same SHA-256). The model and the test pilot changed. Some changes were fitted to the same published sources the author used for the cards; the cards that rest on those sources are therefore no longer independent checks of them.

Before the fourth flight (release 2.2)

  • The flight model is unchanged. The fourth flight checks the test pilot, which the third evaluator found off the plan in three places, and gives the last open items their final reading (the developer's notes in the card tables).
  • H8a and H8b, vortex-ring entry: the recovery now starts at the plan's 1,800 fpm of vertical descent. Until 2.1 the pilot started it at the first of 1,750 fpm or a ring depth of 0.8, which cut the entry at ~1,530 fpm.
  • H8b, classical recovery: 10° nose down from the entry attitude, as the plan says (until 2.1 to −10°, about 13° below it).
  • F6, autorotation: the collective stays full down after the failure, as the plan says. Until 2.1 the autopilot's autorotation mode lifted it a little to hold NR near 100 %.
  • H6, recording: a doubling time from the growth of successive extrema is added. The old measure, the time at which an extremum first reached twice the first one (14.2 s), read like a doubling time but is not one; the evaluator had computed 4.8 s from the record.
  • H8a and H8b, recording: the thrust fluctuation is also given without the slow change from the collective step and the deepening descent (thrust minus its 4-s running mean), as the qualities suite measures the ring's roughness.

Before the third flight (release 2.1)

  • Fuselage side force by the crossflow principle, calibrated to Kampa et al. 1997, Fig. 18 (the EC135 derivative breakdown; the same paper is a source of several cards). Until 2.0 the cabin and tail-boom strips took the whole airspeed into their crossflow, which made the fuselage weathercock-stable (+0.06 m³/°) and gave it a yaw damping of −0.25 1/s at 90 kt; Kampa gives −0.26 m³/° (destabilising) and about zero. Now each strip feels only the flow across the body, the cabin adds a low-aspect-ratio side force near the CG, and the fuselage yaw moment volume was set to the published value. This is the change behind the longer Dutch-roll period (F3, F4).
  • Yaw inertia 4900 kg m², calibrated again to the DLR Dutch roll at 60 kt (the source of F4.3; 6300 kg m² in 1.9, fitted then to a fuselage that damped yaw by itself).
  • Fin: camber 0.83 and the centre of pressure 1.45 m above the CG. The camber is set with Airbus IN 3540 (zero Fenestron thrust in ball-centred cruise at 120 kt; source of F1.6 and H1.4); the height with the fin-and-Fenestron dihedral effect of Kampa Fig. 18 (−0.12 m³/°).
  • Fenestron. Physics: reverse thrust now comes from the fan's own disc area (until 2.0 a quarter of it, which in cruise, where the fan runs near zero thrust, cancelled most of its response to sideslip); the duct's augmentation fades faster with flight speed across the duct than with the rotor downwash. Calibrated: the right-stop fan pitch, −11° (1.9: −25°), set again with IN 3540 (reverse thrust near the right stop in autorotation; source of F6.4), since the corrected relation needs less pitch for the same reverse thrust.
  • Rotor blades twist under stall (physics, parameters estimated): the separated-flow pitching moment of stalled sections twists the blade nose-down through the torsional stiffness of blade and control path, so a deep stall limits itself instead of rolling the aircraft over (F7).
  • Fuselage vertical force by the same crossflow principle plus the cabin's lift: at 140 kt the fuselage no longer presses down with 1.6 kN that the rotor had to carry. The frontal drag area was fitted again to 137 kt at maximum continuous power (1.67 m²; a calibration as before).
  • Drive train: gear mesh losses (2.5 %), accessories on the main gearbox 4 kW (the generators sit on the engines and leave the rotor with them after a double engine failure, H7), and the FADEC sees the load through a 0.15 s filter. The turbine lapse was fitted again to the published hover ceilings (a calibration as before).
  • Test pilot: heading changes are now summed along the record. Until 1.9 they were taken modulo 360°, so a spin of −545° (H7) was recorded as +175°.

Before the second flight (release 1.9)

  • Test pilot, balance technique. In the first flight the autopilot that trims each condition turned the ball toward the centre so slowly that it never got there within its minute, and its pedal integrator stopped at 0.4: the high-speed pedal positions of F1 (and H1.4) were partly this limit. Now the bank holds the heading and the pedals keep the nose on the flight path at 40 kt and centre the ball from 80 kt, blended in between. Affects F1, F6, F9 and every trim.
  • Fin camber, calibrated to Airbus IN 3540 (the source of F1.6, F6.4 and H1.4): the fin now carries about two thirds of the anti-torque in balanced cruise. With it, the Fenestron pedal gearing got a larger reverse range (right stop −25° instead of −10°) so that the steady autorotation can be balanced short of the stop.
  • Rotor polar inertia 2000 kg m², calibrated to the Airbus simulation quoted in AAIB AAR 3/2015 (also a source of H7 and F6): 1350 kg m² before.
  • Yaw inertia 6300 kg m², calibrated to the DLR Dutch roll at 60 kt (the source of F4.3): 5000 kg m² before.
  • Vortex-ring roughness: slower, and no longer cancelled by the model's own recirculation feedback; its amplitude was set with the 10–40 % band of H8a.3 in view.
  • Physics without calibration: dynamic stall of the rotor blades (F7); the Fenestron duct's ram drag and the rotor downwash across the fuselage (hover yaw damping, H4); the frontal drag area re-fitted to 137 kt at maximum continuous power once the Fenestron no longer absorbed 50–60 kW there.
  • Recordings: rotor thrust and engine power are now one-revolution averages (the four-per-revolution of the rotor aliased into ±12 kN jumps at 20 Hz), and the lateral specific force (the slip ball) is recorded.

The finding that came first

Writing the cards, the author noticed that the plan described the EC135's main rotor as turning clockwise seen from above, the way the simulator had it since its first version. It turns counterclockwise, like the BO105 and BK117 it descends from: Airbus Helicopters Information Notice IN 3540-I-00 lists the EC135 P2+ among the types with a counterclockwise rotor, and the flight manual describes a double engine failure as a "yawing motion nose left". Every effect that depends on the sense was mirrored: the pedal that goes with power, the low skid in the hover, the yaw at a power loss, the retreating side. The model was corrected before the flight, and the cards were not touched.

Main discrepancies

  1. H7 (red flag, medium confidence): after a hover double engine failure with the lever fully down at 2.1 s, NR keeps falling at about 2.4 %/s to a minimum of 74.4 % at 4.0 s (expected 72-90 % at 1.3-2.5 s). It is still falling 1.3 s after the descent passed 2,000 fpm and is only 75.8 % at 5 s (expected 85-105 %); the rotor begins to recover only near 3,300 fpm of descent.
  2. F1 (medium confidence): the high-speed end of the power curve is steep relative to the hover. P(135 kt)/P(hover) = 1.32 (expected 0.8-1.2) and the 135 kt collective is +4.0 deg above hover (expected at most +2.5 deg), although every absolute power point, from 442 kW in the hover (bottom of band) to 584 kW at 135 kt, is inside its band.
  3. H4 (low confidence): after the right pedal is returned to trim at 4 s, the hover yaw rate drops from 37.9 to 27 deg/s in 1 s and then stalls at about 25 deg/s (66 % left after 2 s; fitted time constant 5.2 s against the expected 1.4-3 s), while about 2 m/s of rightward drift builds.
  4. H2/H3 (low confidence): pure cyclic pulses in the hover produce uncommanded nose-right yaw of 8.8 deg/s (H2, at 5.5 s) and 7.4 deg/s (H3, at 6 s) against the expected |r| < 5 deg/s, growing with 2-2.4 m/s of lateral drift. The hover yaw axis looks strongly coupled to sideways drift.
  5. F3 (low confidence): the yaw during the 100 kt roll-in is proverse (r up to +1.2 deg/s nose right) instead of the adverse nose-left yaw expected from the identified EC135 Np < 0. The turn rate at 4-8 s (0.9-1.4 deg/s) is at the bottom of the expected +1..+5 deg/s.
  6. F7 (low confidence on magnitude): the retreating-blade-stall roll is in the right direction but very large, p -57.5 deg/s and a phi change of -59.5 deg at 2.7 s against the predicted -5..-35 deg, with about 41 % of the disc staying stalled for 2.6 s after the stick returns.
  7. F9/trim (medium confidence): with the ball centred the model flies at +3.4..+3.8 deg sideslip in level flight at 80-100 kt and +3.55 deg in the 30 deg turn, against the expected |beta| <= 3 deg.
  8. F6 (minor): the NR minimum (83.7 %) comes at 2.85 s instead of 1.2-2.5 s because of the pilot's pull-up from -0.23 g. Steady autorotation NR is 110.75 %, above the 106 % power-off limit for the whole of 5.6-40 s (inside the card's 95-112 % band).

These are the open items after the fourth flight, in the evaluator's words. Where the developer thinks a discrepancy comes from the physics of the type rather than from the model, the card table says why, marked Developer; that is the developer's reading, not the evaluator's. The cards stay frozen, so the same flight test can be flown again after each change and the result compared.

Method

Method: expectations written blind from published data and textbook physics, without access to the simulator. Numbers come from EC135-specific sources where they exist (Kampa et al. ERF 1997 for rotor stiffness, roll damping and control sensitivity, bandwidth and blade-loading limits; DLR ACT/FHS system identification for derivatives and identified modes; EASA TCDS and RFM extracts in AAIB AAR 3/2015 for limits, rotor decay and autorotation; Airbus safety and information notices), and otherwise from the BO-105 analogue (NASA CR-3144 derivatives) and the author's momentum/linear-model calculations. Ranges are deliberately wide where only analogues exist. IMPORTANT, ROTOR DIRECTION: the plan says the EC135 main rotor turns clockwise seen from above. That is wrong for the real aircraft. Airbus SIN 3298-S-00 lists the EC135 P2+ under 'main rotor rotating counter clockwise'. Airbus IN 3540-I-00 groups the EC135 with the BO105 and BK117 as helicopters 'with a main rotor rotating counterclockwise when seen from above'. The EC135 RFM (quoted in AAIB AAR 3/2015 App. A) gives 'yawing motion nose left' at double engine failure, which only fits a counterclockwise rotor. The DLR-identified hover derivative Lu > 0 (forward speed gives right roll, i.e. right transverse-flow roll) is also consistent with a counterclockwise rotor. All cards are therefore written for the real counterclockwise rotor: hangs left skid low, left pedal with power, nose right with collective, nose left at power loss, retreating blade on the left. Every expectation whose sign depends on the rotor direction is tagged [ROT]. If the model was built with the clockwise rotor stated in the plan, all [ROT] lateral and directional signs will mirror. That would be a configuration error against the real EC135, not necessarily a dynamics error, and the evaluator should report it as such. Off-axis (pitch-roll) coupling signs of this bearingless rotor are not predicted: they depend on the unpublished swashplate phase angle and on whether the rotor lies in the BO105-like regime. Only bounds are given for them. Input conversions used: lat 0.10 = 0.75 deg = 8 mm; lon 0.10 = 1.09 deg; col 0.05 = 0.73 deg. Pedal gearing is unpublished, so pedal-driven magnitudes are low confidence.

Each card's expectations were compared with the recorded metrics and, wherever a metric did not match the card's quantity, with values computed from the 20 Hz series (scripts in /tmp/ft-eval22/calc/): values at given times, peak values and times, first-order and exponential fits for time constants, zero crossings and extrema for periods and doubling times, parabola fits for the power-bucket minimum, and unwrapped heading. Numbers just outside a range (within about 25 % of the range width or of the bound) were graded NEAR; wrong sign or shape, or a number further out, was graded FAIL. Peak times on a flat plateau that extends into the window were not penalised. Red flags were checked one by one. Deviations in how cards were flown (F6, H8a/H8b from 4,000 ft; F1 pedal logic; T1 and F9 pilot laws) are noted where they matter.

Plots show what the scripted pilot recorded at 20 Hz. Airspeed in the vortex-ring cards is the horizontal component, since the indicated airspeed of a vertical descent is mostly sink rate.
